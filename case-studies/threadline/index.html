Case study 03 · Product analytics

Threadline

A product-discovery analysis that turns ordered clickstream events into session-depth metrics, path diagnostics, a transparent review queue, and a statistically grounded experiment plan.

The decision

Should the product-listing experience change to help more sessions reach meaningful multi-item exploration without increasing short sessions?

AudienceProduct, growth, design, experimentation teams
SourceUCI clickstream · Apr–Aug 2008
Analytical scope165,474 events · 24,026 sessions
StackSQL · Python · dbt · Power analysis
01 Problem02 Data03 Approach04 Findings05 Experiment06 Explore07 Technical proof
01

Problem

The event stream records browsing but no cart or purchase event. The challenge was to make a useful product recommendation without inventing a conversion funnel the data does not contain.

The work therefore focuses on observable browsing depth, ordered category movement, placement behavior, and transparent diagnostic rules. Behavioral thresholds are labeled as proxies, not as purchase intent.

Question 01

How deeply do sessions explore the product catalog?

Question 02

Where do paths, repeated clicks, or reversals suggest investigation?

Question 03

What experiment could improve discovery, and how much traffic would it require?

02

Data

The source contains ordered product-page views grouped into anonymous browsing sessions.

Required analytical fields are complete, and every `(session_id, click_order)` key is unique. Event order supports path analysis, but timestamps, dwell time, device, performance, cart, checkout, and purchase events are absent.

165,474Ordered product-view events
24,026Distinct browsing sessions
217Products across four categories
Evidence boundary

“Exploration,” “consideration,” “deep exploration,” and “friction” are documented behavioral proxies. They are not observed purchase intent, conversion, user frustration, or causal diagnoses.

Source: Łapczyński, M. & Białowąs, S. (2013), Clickstream Data for Online Shopping, UCI Machine Learning Repository, CC BY 4.0. The historical period supports method demonstration, not claims about current commerce behavior.

03

Approach

The project separates what was observed, what was derived, what was flagged for review, and what would need to be tested experimentally.

Diagnose

05

Experiment

Define hypothesis, randomization, metrics, guardrails, power, and readout discipline.

Proxy discipline

Two, four, and seven product views define exploration thresholds. The four-view “consideration” metric is a depth proxy selected for experiment planning—not a renamed conversion.

Instrumentation critique

A production implementation should add add-to-cart, checkout, purchase, device, performance, exposure, and error events before evaluating downstream business impact.

04

Findings

The data shows substantial multi-item browsing, but it also exposes concentration and ambiguity that should shape the next test.

6.9

Average views per session indicate meaningful catalog exploration.

Seventy-nine percent of sessions view at least two products, 54.3% reach the four-view consideration proxy, and 33.3% reach seven views.

21.0%

One-click sessions form an important guardrail.

A short recorded session is a diagnostic, not necessarily a web-analytics bounce. Any discovery treatment should avoid increasing this rate.

17.1%

A transparent rule narrows the friction-review queue.

Repeated clicks, page reversals, or narrow long browsing trigger investigation. Representative paths should be replayed before proposing a design change.

81.5%

Geographic concentration limits generalization.

Poland contributes most sessions, so aggregate behavior is not a balanced global benchmark. Country segments should be declared before experimentation.

05

Experiment

Recommended test

Test clearer category cues and product-comparison affordances with session-level random assignment.

Use the share of sessions reaching four product views as the primary metric. Track one-click session rate, average views, page reversals, and—once instrumented—performance and error rate as guardrails.

  • Control: current product-listing experience. Treatment: revised category cues and comparison affordance.
  • At the 54.3% baseline, a 10% relative lift requires about 1,305 sessions per variant.
  • Observed traffic suggests roughly 15 days, subject to recalculation with current eligible production traffic.
  • Use intent-to-treat analysis, report confidence intervals, test sample-ratio mismatch, and avoid early stopping.
