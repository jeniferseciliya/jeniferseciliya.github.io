Case study 01 · GTM analytics

SignalDeck

A public-signal GTM intelligence system that helps an AI-cloud sales organization decide which accounts merit research, how to balance territory capacity, and whether the metric underneath the dashboard can be trusted.

The decision

Where should GTM operators investigate first, how should they distribute account potential, and which records require review before any sales action?

AudienceGTM leadership, RevOps, Sales Operations
SourcesGreenhouse · GitHub · Hugging Face

01

Problem

AI appears in thousands of company pages, job descriptions, and repositories. The GTM problem is deciding which signals deserve attention without converting public activity into imaginary pipeline.

A useful system had to separate observable technical investment from private buying intent, exclude direct infrastructure providers from a prospect-planning scenario, make every score explainable, and expose identity or grain problems before leadership used the output.

Question 01

Which accounts show multiple, current technical-investment signals?

Question 02

Can four territories be balanced by potential rather than account count?

Question 03

Is the dashboard showing a decision-ready metric or merely clean-looking noise?

02

Data

The first release monitors 25 real companies across AI-native builders, enterprise adopters, cloud software, and infrastructure market references.

Public Greenhouse boards supply current openings. GitHub organizations provide public repository activity, and Hugging Face organizations provide public model activity where available. Full job descriptions are used temporarily for classification but are not republished.

Evidence boundary

Hiring and open-source activity indicate observable technical investment. They do not prove budget, purchase intent, sales qualification, pipeline, bookings, ARR, customer status, or vendor evaluation.

Sources: public Greenhouse Job Board, GitHub organization, and Hugging Face Hub endpoints, observed August 1, 2026 UTC. The curated panel is not a complete market census.

03

Approach

The system was designed like a governed RevOps product: define the account grain, resolve identities, classify evidence, build metric contracts, test reconciliation, then expose a planning scenario.

01

Collect

Retrieve public job, repository, and model signals without redistributing full descriptions.

02

Resolve

Map company identities across sources and preserve a confidence-based review queue.

03

Score

Combine panel-relative hiring, open-source, and public-model evidence transparently.

04

Plan

Exclude market references and distribute eligible accounts across anonymous territories.

05

Validate

Test account grain, job grain, relationships, bounds, categories, and planning exclusions.

Metric design

The GTM priority score is 45% hiring, 30% public open-source, and 25% public-model signal. Component metrics use within-panel percentile ranks so raw company size does not automatically force every large company to the score ceiling.

Responsible AI

Rules and tested marts calculate every number. AI may draft a weekly briefing or propose exception resolutions, but it cannot invent CRM measures, silently resolve an identity, or publish without human review.

04

Findings

The useful outcome is a defensible research queue and a visible set of operating tradeoffs—not a claim that public behavior predicts a sale.

31.7%

A strict rule removed most general openings from the research signal.

1,866 of 5,886 public openings qualified through an explicit AI/ML title or a technical title paired with concrete GPU, distributed-compute, training, serving, or ML-platform context.

6

Six eligible accounts reached the strategic research band.

The threshold requires a score of at least 50 and evidence from two or more source families. Infrastructure providers remain visible as market references but are excluded from territory assignments.

24.8%

Equal account counts did not produce equal territory potential.

The transparent four-territory baseline still has a 24.8% max-to-min weighted-potential spread, showing why capacity reviews need more than a count of logos.

27/27

Metric checks passed while three identities remained visibly unresolved.

Account and posting grains reconcile with no missing titles or source URLs. Medium-confidence source mappings stay in a human review queue rather than disappearing behind a green dashboard.

05

Decision

Recommended operating use

Use SignalDeck to prioritize account research and territory discussion—then join the result with governed CRM evidence before activation.

The public layer is appropriate for market sensing and research prioritization. Leads, contacts, opportunity stages, ownership, conversion, ARR, bookings, quota, and forecast measures must remain in the internal Salesforce and revenue-data environment.

  • Research strategic-band accounts and validate workload fit before outreach.
  • Resolve medium-confidence identities before creating or enriching CRM records.
  • Rebalance territories with actual capacity, ownership, quota, geography, and segment constraints.
  • Use an AI-written briefing only when every claim links back to a tested metric and human reviewer.

07

Technical proof

The artifact exposes the source map, classification rules, SQL models, metric contracts, tests, planning assumptions, and AI review boundaries behind the visual layer.

Validation

The published build completed 8 dbt models and 19 data tests—27 of 27 operations—with zero warnings or errors. Tests cover grain, relationships, accepted values, score bounds, and market-reference exclusions.

GTM judgment

The project explicitly distinguishes public investment signals from private CRM measures and labels the territory output as a scenario. This prevents impressive-looking charts from silently overstating what the source can prove.
