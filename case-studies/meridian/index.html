Case study 02 · Business intelligence

Meridian

A retail BI system that turns a messy transaction workbook into governed revenue metrics, customer lifecycle views, product signals, and a reviewable exception-monitoring workflow.

The decision

Where is commercial performance coming from, what threatens its quality or durability, and which exceptions deserve an analyst’s attention first?

01

Problem

Retail reporting becomes unreliable when revenue, cancellations, customer identity, and partial periods are handled as presentation details instead of metric contracts.

The project needed to create a trustworthy executive view while preserving the distinctions between completed sales and cancellations, commercial metrics and customer metrics, descriptive signals and causal explanations.

Question 01

How are revenue, orders, and customer activity changing?

Question 02

Where are geography, product, and cancellation risks concentrated?

Question 03

Which unusual days merit investigation rather than automatic explanation?

02

Data

The source is a real two-sheet retail workbook containing more than one million transaction lines from a UK-based online retailer.

Each line includes invoice, product, quantity, date, unit price, customer identifier, and country. The quality assessment preserves material exclusions instead of hiding them behind a single “cleaned” table.

Evidence boundary

Missing customer IDs remain in commercial KPIs but are excluded from retention and RFM analysis. Cancellations are modeled separately. December 2011 ends on December 9, so it is not treated as a complete month.

03

Approach

A reusable analytical layer was built before the dashboard so every view shared the same definitions.

01

Profile

Assess dates, customer gaps, cancellation markers, prices, quantities, and duplicate-looking rows.

02

Normalize

Standardize workbook sheets into a documented transaction layer.

03

Model

Separate completed sales and cancellations; create KPI, product, RFM, and cohort marts.

04

Monitor

Flag unusual revenue days against a robust centered 28-day baseline.

05

Validate

Test positive sales, cancellation logic, uniqueness, accepted values, and metric bounds.

Metric design

Net sales require non-cancelled invoices, positive quantity, positive price, and a valid date. Customer lifecycle metrics use only known customer IDs, while commercial KPIs retain valid anonymous sales.

Exception logic

A robust z-score identifies days worth reviewing. A signal means “investigate,” not “a business problem occurred,” and every disposition remains a human analytical decision.

04

Findings

The commercial picture combines strong scale with meaningful concentration and operational questions.

£20.97M

Completed positive-value sales span 40,077 orders.

The governed revenue metric excludes cancellations and invalid values rather than silently netting unlike transaction states together.

85.2%

Revenue is heavily concentrated in the United Kingdom.

The domestic base provides scale, but portfolio-wide growth can obscure limited geographic diversification and different operational economics.

22.6%

Cancellation exposure merits an operating review.

The rate is an order-level source signal. High-value cancellations, repeat-cancellation customers, products, and country differences should be investigated before causes are assigned.

20 days

Robust exception monitoring narrows the investigation queue.

Each flagged day should be traced to invoices, products, countries, and data-quality events, then classified as a valid event, operational issue, or source problem.

05

Decision

Recommended action

Protect the core revenue engine while investigating concentration, cancellations, and the quality of recent growth.

November 2011 revenue rose 30.7% from October, but the increase should be decomposed before it is treated as durable. Product, country, order-size, and new-versus-repeat-customer contributions can reveal what is actually driving it.

  • Trace high-value and repeat cancellations to products, customers, countries, and source-system rules.
  • Protect hero-product availability and examine basket affinity and return behavior.
  • Evaluate international growth with acquisition, retention, fulfilment cost, and cancellation context.
  • Record the disposition of every anomaly instead of auto-generating a cause.
