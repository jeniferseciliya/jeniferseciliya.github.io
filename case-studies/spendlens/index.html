Case study 01 · Finance operations

SpendLens

A self-service finance control tower that turns real purchasing-card activity into department visibility, explainable review priorities, and an evidence contract for recurring AI-assisted reporting.

The decision

Where should Finance focus limited review time—and what can department leaders investigate without waiting for an analyst?

AudienceFinance, Operations, Accounting, department leaders
SourceDelaware Department of Finance · monthly open data
Analytical grain1,442,956 published card transactions

01

Problem

A finance team processing more than a million card records cannot investigate every transaction with equal attention. A useful analytical product must narrow the review surface without turning unusual activity into an accusation.

The challenge was to establish a trustworthy transaction grain, reconcile credits and fiscal periods, normalize unstable merchant labels, give departments a self-service view, and explain exactly why a record entered the review queue.

Question 01

Which departments, categories, and merchants drive current spend?

Question 02

Which transaction contexts merit scarce Finance review time?

Question 03

Can a recurring narrative remain grounded in tested metrics?

02

Data

The State of Delaware publishes real employee purchasing-card activity with fiscal period, department, division, merchant, merchant category, transaction date, and amount.

The July 10, 2026 extract contains records from March 2017 through June 2026. It provides enough organizational and merchant detail for operational reporting, while excluding cardholder identities, receipts, approvals, budgets, contract identifiers, and policy outcomes.

Evidence boundary

The source can support spend monitoring and review prioritization. It cannot determine whether a purchase was authorized, documented, necessary, fraudulent, wasteful, or policy compliant.

Source: State of Delaware Department of Finance, Division of Accounting. The current export contains fiscal years 2018–2024 and 2026; FY2025 is absent and is not imputed.

03

Approach

SpendLens was designed as an operational BI product: preserve source truth, build governed metrics, surface data-quality friction, create explainable review rules, then expose role-appropriate self-service views.

01

Ingest

Load the monthly official CSV into a reproducible DuckDB raw layer.

02

Normalize

Preserve raw merchant text while resolving common processor and vendor variants.

03

Model

Build transaction, department-period, category, merchant, and baseline marts.

04

Prioritize

Score transparent amount, merchant-day, new-merchant, weekend, and credit signals.

05

Control

Reconcile totals, test every grain, and draft narrative only from an evidence packet.

Review logic

High-amount signals use each department-category P99 with a minimum history requirement. Merchant-day clusters create one representative queue item, preventing repeated activity from inflating the operational workload.

Responsible AI

SQL calculates every metric. The dashboard assembles a structured packet and controlled narrative draft; a model may assist with wording but cannot invent a number, infer intent, or publish without human review.

04

Findings

The strongest findings are operational: inconsistent merchant identity and reconciliation timing materially change how a finance dashboard must be designed.

39.4%

Merchant normalization removed substantial label fragmentation.

308,884 raw merchant strings resolve to 187,088 analytical entities. Both versions remain available so an analyst can audit every mapping decision.

15.1%

Transaction month and fiscal period do not always align.

217,339 records appear in a later fiscal period than their transaction month; 215,014 lag by one period. The dashboard therefore distinguishes operational transaction timing from fiscal reporting.

1.88%

A small, explainable queue replaces blanket manual review.

27,109 records meet at least one review rule and 1,429 receive high priority. The queue states why each record surfaced without claiming fraud or noncompliance.

42/42

All models and data-quality checks pass.

Tests cover source and fact grain, required fields, fiscal values, posting lag, credit definitions, review bounds, signal reasons, and aggregate reconciliation.

05

Decision

Recommended operating use

Use SpendLens as a triage and self-service reporting layer—then resolve flagged records with internal receipts, approvals, policies, budgets, and cardholder context.

The public-data implementation demonstrates the analytical operating model. A production deployment would join the reviewed queue to governed expense, HRIS, ERP, budget, receipt, and approval sources before any compliance decision.

  • Give department leaders filtered visibility into their own spend drivers.
  • Route high-priority signals to Finance with a reason and comparison baseline.
  • Monitor late-posting behavior so fiscal and operational reports reconcile.
  • Generate recurring summaries only from tested metrics and require analyst approval.

07

Technical proof

The repository exposes the source contract, merchant rules, percentile baselines, score thresholds, SQL models, tests, reconciliation logic, analytical outputs, and AI reporting boundary behind the visual layer.

Validation

The build completed 9 dbt models and 33 tests—42 of 42 operations—with no errors or warnings. Fact totals reconcile to the department-period mart.

Finance judgment

Credits remain visible, exact source rows are not silently deduplicated, missing FY2025 coverage is disclosed, fiscal lag is modeled, and no review signal is presented as an adjudicated outcome.
