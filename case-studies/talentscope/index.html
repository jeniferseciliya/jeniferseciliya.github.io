Case study 01 · People analytics

TalentScope

A workforce-intelligence analysis that asks how recruiting and compensation teams should read Bay Area market pressure from public labor filings—without pretending a filing is a hire.

The decision

Where should a people team focus recruiting attention, how should it benchmark salary floors, and where does employer concentration create competitive pressure?

AudiencePeople, recruiting, compensation, workforce planning
SourceU.S. DOL OFLC · FY2025
Analytical grain594,821 latest resolved cases
StackSQL · Python · dbt · DuckDB
01 Problem02 Data03 Approach04 Findings05 Recommendation06 Explore07 Technical proof
01

Problem

Public labor data is rich enough to inform market strategy, but easy to mislabel. The analytical challenge was to extract useful recruiting and compensation signals while staying honest about what the source can prove.

A useful answer had to distinguish cases from positions, filing activity from confirmed hiring, and salary floors from realized compensation. It also had to resolve repeated case determinations before any market comparison could be trusted.

Question 01

Where is specialized talent demand concentrated?

Question 02

How do Bay Area salary floors compare with national role benchmarks?

Question 03

Which employers and occupations shape the competitive market?

02

Data

Four official FY2025 disclosure workbooks contributed 596,552 rows of LCA certification activity.

The source contains filing status, requested worker positions, job and employer information, worksite geography, wage floors, prevailing wages, and processing dates. Personal and representative contact fields were excluded from the analytical extract.

Evidence boundary

LCA filings are a specialized-labor demand proxy—not job openings, applicants, offers, or confirmed hires. Salary figures are annualized filing lower bounds, not total compensation or pay-equity outcomes.

Source: U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration, Office of Foreign Labor Certification, FY2025 disclosure files.

03

Approach

The analysis was designed as a governed pipeline, not a spreadsheet calculation feeding a chart.

01

Acquire

Ingest four official quarterly workbooks and preserve source lineage.

02

Profile

Reconcile schema drift and remove unnecessary personal fields.

03

Resolve

Keep the latest determination per case using date and status priority.

04

Model

Annualize wages, define markets and role families, and build reusable marts.

05

Validate

Test grain, geography, wage bounds, accepted values, and business rules.

Metric design

Certified positions use requested worker positions on certified latest outcomes. Market salary delta compares a segment median with the same role family nationally, while preserving the composition caveat.

Responsible AI

Rules calculate the evidence. AI may help draft a decision brief, but it cannot calculate metrics, invent causes, remove limitations, or publish without human review.

04

Findings

The most useful result was not a single market ranking—it was a set of signals a people team could use to ask better planning questions.

$139,214

Bay Area People & Talent carries a substantial salary-floor premium.

The segment median is 64.2% above the national People & Talent median. This is a market and role-mix comparison, not a cost-of-living or pay-equity estimate.

54.4%

Demand is concentrated among a small employer group.

The ten largest employers account for 54.4% of certified positions in Bay Area People & Talent, compared with 29.6% nationally.

11,351

Data & Analytics is an adjacent, higher-volume talent market.

Bay Area Data & Analytics includes 5,465 new-employment positions and a $165,000 median salary floor—36.8% above its national role-family median.

05

Decision

Recommended use

Use the analysis as an external market lens, then join it with internal recruiting evidence before making workforce decisions.

The filing data is strongest for market mapping and compensation context. It becomes more decision-ready when combined with internal requisition, applicant-stage, offer, acceptance, time-to-fill, and retention data.

  • Review salary bands where external filing floors materially exceed internal expectations.
  • Prioritize sourcing research in markets with both demand volume and employer concentration.
  • Track filing operations separately from recruiting funnel performance.
  • Validate market signals with current internal ATS and compensation data before acting.
